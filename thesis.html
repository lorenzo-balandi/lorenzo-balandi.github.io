PhD Thesis

Thesis Abstract

Aerial physical interaction promises to bring robotic dexterity to hardly accessible locations, but extending it beyond quasi-static tasks requires advances in design and control. This thesis addresses agile flight under uncertainty and advanced aerial interaction through a system-level approach combining hardware development with model-based and learning-based control.

First, an optimization-based inner-loop controller combines acceleration feedback and actuation constraints to improve tracking under uncertainty, supported by a novel vibration damping system for onboard electronics.

The thesis then presents an aerial manipulator with a torque-controlled arm and a whole-body nonlinear model predictive controller. Extensive experiments demonstrate agile motion, collision avoidance, versatile contact exploitation, force tracking, and transparent physical interaction with humans.

Finally, learning-based control is investigated and a policy for full-pose control is trained using reinforcement learning and deployed on a fully actuated aerial vehicle without further adaptation, directly commanding rotor velocities. The framework is extended to non-prehensile aerial manipulation, with preliminary sim-to-sim validation.
